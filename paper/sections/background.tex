\subsection{Mutation-Based SQLi Attacks}
Adversarial methods search for inputs that preserve malicious intent while evading detectors. Reinforcement learning approaches like SSQLi \cite{guan2023ssqli}, GAN-based methods like GSQLi \cite{khan2024gsqli}, and semantics-preserving fuzzing like WAF-A-MoLE \cite{demetrio2020wafamole} achieve high bypass rates. The underlying attack space is combinatorial, involving comment insertions, encodings, and dialect-specific spellings that interact non-monotonically with tokenized defenses \cite{mutation-sqli}. Consequently, the relevant metric is not just the aggregate bypass rate, but which specific compositions survive, as this informs both attacker and defender strategies.

\subsection{Parsing Discrepancies and Defense Evaluation}
When components disagree on decoding order or structural boundaries, attackers can satisfy edge predicates while delivering malicious semantics \cite{akhavani2025waffled}. WAFFLED demonstrated this across major WAFs. Our experiments stabilize parameter binding via remapped seeds to isolate representation-level variance. 

Hybrid defenses aim to raise evasion costs through better data \cite{elebiary2026wamm}, shadow models \cite{wu2025wafbooster}, or preprocessing \cite{zhang2026bwafsql}. However, evaluating these defenses requires paired offensive benchmarks. Without logs tying attempts to mutation families, it is difficult to determine if a mitigation removes high-yield operators or merely reshuffles the search order. Our work supplies a log-grounded baseline for such comparisons.

\subsection{Metrics and Confounding Threats}
Black-box evaluation relies on observable HTTP outcomes. Since different probes map the same vulnerability to different observables, any operational definition of a bypass embeds epistemic commitments. Transparent scripting and published logs mitigate this issue, though they do not eliminate it entirely.
